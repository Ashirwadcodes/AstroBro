[Lagna] The Lagna (ascendant) is the zodiac sign rising on the eastern horizon at the moment and place of birth. In whole-sign houses the Lagna sign is the first house and each following sign is the next house. Traditionally the Lagna is read as the body and overall orientation of the chart.

[Rashi] The Rashi is one of twelve 30-degree divisions of the sidereal zodiac, from Mesha to Meena. The Moon's Rashi is called the Janma Rashi and is central in Vedic astrology, unlike Western astrology which stresses the Sun sign.

[Nakshatra] There are 27 Nakshatras, each spanning 13 degrees 20 minutes of the sidereal zodiac and divided into four padas. The Moon's Nakshatra at birth (Janma Nakshatra) determines the starting point of the Vimshottari Dasha.

[Vimshottari Dasha] Vimshottari is a 120-year planetary period system. The sequence is Ketu 7, Venus 20, Sun 6, Moon 10, Mars 7, Rahu 18, Jupiter 16, Saturn 19, Mercury 17 years. The first period is that of the lord of the Moon's birth Nakshatra, reduced by the fraction of the Nakshatra already traversed by the Moon.

[Ayanamsa] The Ayanamsa is the angular offset between the tropical and sidereal zodiacs, caused by precession. Lahiri (Chitrapaksha) is the standard used in India. Different Ayanamsas can place a planet in a different Rashi, so a chart must record which was used.

[Graha] The nine Grahas are Surya (Sun), Chandra (Moon), Mangala (Mars), Budha (Mercury), Guru (Jupiter), Shukra (Venus), Shani (Saturn), and the lunar nodes Rahu and Ketu. Rahu and Ketu are mathematical points, not bodies.

[Karma and Jyotish] Many practitioners understand the chart as indicating tendencies shaped by past karma, while holding that present action remains free. This is an interpretive tradition and not a scientifically established mechanism. Compare the Gita's emphasis on action without attachment to fruit.

[Limits] Jyotish interpretation varies between lineages. Nothing here is a prediction or a substitute for medical, legal, financial or psychological advice.

[Navamsa] The Navamsa (D9) divides each sign into nine parts of 3 degrees 20 minutes. Counting runs on continuously through the zodiac, so the first part of a fire sign is Mesha, of an earth sign Makara, of an air sign Tula, and of a water sign Karka. Many practitioners read the Navamsa as a deeper layer of a planet's strength and as the chart of partnership. A planet in the same sign in the Rashi and Navamsa charts is called vargottama and is traditionally considered strengthened.

[Antardasha] An Antardasha is a sub-period inside a Mahadasha. Its length is the Mahadasha length multiplied by the sub-lord's years and divided by 120, and the sub-periods run in the same order, starting with the Mahadasha lord itself. A Pratyantardasha divides an Antardasha the same way. For the first period of a life, the sub-periods are laid out for the full period and the part before birth is simply skipped.

[Drishti] Drishti means a planet's aspect, or glance, on a house. In the common whole-sign method every planet aspects the 7th house from itself. Mars also aspects the 4th and 8th, Jupiter the 5th and 9th, and Saturn the 3rd and 10th. Schools differ on whether Rahu and Ketu aspect, so this project does not compute node aspects.

[Yogas] A yoga is a named planetary combination. Examples with simple definitions: Gajakesari is Jupiter in a kendra (1st, 4th, 7th, 10th house) from the Moon; Budha-Aditya is the Sun and Mercury in one sign; the five Pancha Mahapurusha yogas arise when Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus or Saturn is in its own or exalted sign and in a kendra from the Lagna. Definitions, conditions and cancellations differ between texts and teachers.

[Mangal dosha] Mangal dosha (Kuja dosha) is traditionally said to arise when Mars is in the 1st, 2nd, 4th, 7th, 8th or 12th house from the Lagna, and some teachers also count it from the Moon or Venus. It is used mainly in marriage matching. Traditions list many cancellations and it is a belief, not an established fact.

[Panchanga] The Panchanga has five parts: tithi (a lunar day, each 12 degrees of the Moon's lead over the Sun), vara (weekday, counted sunrise to sunrise), nakshatra, yoga (from the sum of the Sun and Moon longitudes, 27 parts), and karana (half a tithi, 60 per lunar month). Because the Vedic day starts at sunrise, someone born before sunrise belongs to the previous weekday.

[Dignity] A planet in its exaltation sign is traditionally called strong and in its debilitation sign weak; its own sign is also considered strong. Exaltation signs: Sun Mesha, Moon Vrishabha, Mars Makara, Mercury Kanya, Jupiter Karka, Venus Meena, Saturn Tula; debilitation is the opposite sign. A planet moving backwards in the sky is retrograde, and a planet very close to the Sun is called combust. Orbs for combustion differ between texts.

[Sade Sati] Sade Sati is the roughly seven and a half year period when Saturn transits the 12th, 1st and 2nd signs from the natal Moon sign. Dhaiya (or Small Panoti) is Saturn in the 4th or 8th sign from the Moon. Many people treat these as testing periods. This is a tradition, not a scientific forecast.
