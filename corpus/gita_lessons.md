[Gita Lesson 0 - How to start] The Bhagavad Gita is 18 chapters (700 verses) inside the Mahabharata, a conversation between Arjuna and Krishna just before a war. A good first pass is one chapter a week with one question in mind: what is Arjuna confused about, and what does Krishna answer? Read chapters 1 and 2 first, then 3 (action), 6 (meditation), 12 (devotion) and 18 (summary). Return to the middle chapters afterwards.

[Gita Lesson 1 - Arjuna Vishada Yoga] Chapter 1 sets the scene: Arjuna sees teachers and relatives on the opposing side and collapses in grief. Learning point: the Gita begins with a human problem, not a doctrine. Ask when duty and love last pulled you in different directions.

[Gita Lesson 2 - Sankhya Yoga] Chapter 2 is the core. Krishna separates the changing body from the unchanging self (2.13, 2.20) and teaches action without clinging to results (2.47). It also describes the person of steady wisdom (sthitaprajna, 2.54-72). Learning point: calm is not inaction; it is acting without being thrown around by outcomes.

[Gita Lesson 3 - Karma Yoga] Chapter 3 answers: if knowledge is higher, why act? Krishna says no one can stay without acting, so act as service, without selfish attachment. One's own duty, even imperfect, is safer than another's (3.35). Learning point: the practical heart of the Gita for daily life.

[Gita Lesson 4 - Jnana Karma Sanyasa Yoga] Chapter 4 traces the tradition of knowledge and says the divine takes birth when dharma declines (4.7-8). Action done as an offering does not bind, and knowledge purifies. Learning point: knowledge and action are linked, not rivals.

[Gita Lesson 5 - Karma Sanyasa Yoga] Chapter 5 compares renouncing action with performing it in the right spirit, and says the wise see both as one path. Learning point: inner renunciation matters more than outer.

[Gita Lesson 6 - Dhyana Yoga] Chapter 6 teaches meditation: posture, moderation in food and sleep, steadying a restless mind with practice and detachment (6.35). Learning point: the mind can be a friend or an enemy (6.5-6).

[Gita Lesson 7 - Jnana Vijnana Yoga] Chapter 7 describes the divine as both lower nature (the elements) and higher nature (the living principle), and lists four kinds of devotees. Learning point: understanding grows through knowledge and experience together.

[Gita Lesson 8 - Akshara Brahma Yoga] Chapter 8 discusses the imperishable and the state of mind at the time of death. Learning point: lifelong thoughts and practice shape the final turn of mind.

[Gita Lesson 9 - Raja Vidya Raja Guhya Yoga] Chapter 9 is called the royal knowledge and royal secret. It stresses simple devotion: a leaf, flower, fruit or water offered with love (9.26). Learning point: devotion needs no wealth or status.

[Gita Lesson 10 - Vibhuti Yoga] Chapter 10 lists the divine glories in the world. Learning point: a way to see the sacred in the ordinary.

[Gita Lesson 11 - Vishvarupa Darshana Yoga] Chapter 11 is the vision of the cosmic form. Arjuna is awed and frightened and asks to see the gentler form again. Learning point: the infinite overwhelms, and people relate to it through a personal face.

[Gita Lesson 12 - Bhakti Yoga] Chapter 12 compares worship of the formless with worship of a form and describes a dear devotee: friendly, free from hatred, even in pain and pleasure (12.13-14). Learning point: devotion shows up as character.

[Gita Lesson 13 - Kshetra Kshetrajna Vibhaga Yoga] Chapter 13 separates the field (body, mind) from the knower of the field. Learning point: you are not only what you can observe about yourself.

[Gita Lesson 14 - Gunatraya Vibhaga Yoga] Chapter 14 explains the three gunas: sattva (clarity), rajas (restless activity), tamas (inertia). Learning point: a vocabulary for moods and tendencies.

[Gita Lesson 15 - Purushottama Yoga] Chapter 15 uses the upside-down tree for worldly existence and names the Supreme Person beyond the perishable and imperishable. Learning point: a short chapter, good for memorising.

[Gita Lesson 16 - Daivasura Sampad Vibhaga Yoga] Chapter 16 contrasts divine qualities (fearlessness, truthfulness, charity, self-control) with demonic ones (arrogance, anger, greed). Learning point: a mirror for self-examination, not for judging others.

[Gita Lesson 17 - Shraddhatraya Vibhaga Yoga] Chapter 17 applies the three gunas to faith, food, sacrifice, austerity and charity. Learning point: even good deeds differ in quality by motive.

[Gita Lesson 18 - Moksha Sanyasa Yoga] Chapter 18 summarises everything, discusses renunciation, and ends with the call to surrender (18.66). Learning point: reread chapter 2 after this and compare.

[Gita Lesson - Terms] Dharma: duty or the order that holds things together. Karma: action and its results. Yoga: a discipline of union. Atman: the self. Brahman: ultimate reality. Guna: quality of nature. Moksha: liberation. Bhakti: devotion. Jnana: knowledge.

[Gita Lesson - Schools] Teachers read the Gita differently: Advaita (Shankara) stresses knowledge and non-duality, Vishishtadvaita (Ramanuja) and Dvaita (Madhva) stress devotion to a personal God. When comparing commentaries, ask which emphasis each picks.

[Gita Lesson - Translations] The Edwin Arnold translation (1885) in this library is poetic and sometimes loose. For word-by-word meaning compare it with a literal translation and the Sanskrit.
